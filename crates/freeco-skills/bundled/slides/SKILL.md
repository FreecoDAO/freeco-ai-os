# Strategic Slides

A pitch strategist who picks the right deck structure for the goal, writes each slide with a proven copy formula, and lays it out as clean HTML slides. Use it for investor decks, sales pitches, product demos and member presentations. The general presentation skill covers delivery and rehearsal; this one covers structure and copy. Adapted from the MIT-licensed slides skill by Next Level Builder (github.com/nextlevelbuilder/ui-ux-pro-max-skill).

## Key Principles

- Choose the structure from the goal and the audience before writing any slide
- One idea per slide, stated as a headline sentence, not a topic label ("Members save 4 hours a week", not "Benefits")
- Match each slide to a formula and an emotion: curiosity on the hook, frustration on the problem, hope on the solution, trust on proof, urgency on the ask
- Every number on a slide has a source; anything unconfirmed is a visible placeholder, never an invented figure
- End on one clear ask: an amount, a meeting, a sign-up

## Deck Structures

- **Seed investor deck (10 slides)**: title, problem, solution, why now, market size, product, business model, traction, team, ask
- **Sales pitch (9 slides)**: hook, the customer's problem, cost of doing nothing, solution, how it works, proof, pricing, objections answered, next step
- **Product demo (6 slides)**: the job to be done, the before, the live flow, the after, what it costs, how to start
- **Sparkline story**: alternate "what is" with "what could be" slide by slide, then close on the new bliss, the future the audience gets if they act

## Copy Formulas

- **PAS**: Problem, Agitate (what it costs them), Solution
- **AIDA**: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action; good for the title and the closing ask
- **FAB**: Feature, Advantage, Benefit; turn every feature into what the user gains
- **Cost of inaction**: what happens in a year if nothing changes
- **Before-After-Bridge**: the world now, the world with the product, and how to get there
- **Proof stack**: metric, then logo or quote, then case study, strongest first

## Layout Techniques

- A 16:9 frame (1920x1080), a 12-column grid, generous margins (at least 96px), body text no smaller than 28px
- One accent colour for the single thing to notice on each slide
- Charts: a bar chart for comparison, a line chart for a trend, and the takeaway written as the chart title
- Speaker notes on every slide with the talking point and the transition sentence

## Pitfalls to Avoid

- Topic-label headlines that make the audience work out the point
- Walls of bullets; if a slide needs more than three, split it
- Market sizes with no source, or a top-down TAM with no bottom-up check
- Ending on "Questions?" instead of the ask
- Decorative stock photos that carry no information
